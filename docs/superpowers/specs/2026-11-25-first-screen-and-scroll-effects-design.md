# jakpotvin.com: a cinematic first screen and three scroll effects

Design, 25 November 2026. Agreed with Jak in conversation, with mockups shown in a browser. The mockups he reacted to were `opening-v2.html` (first-screen options A, B, C; he chose B) and `scroll-effects-v3.html` (effects 1 to 4; he chose 1, 3 and 4) in the session's scratch folder, which is not kept; where this text and the mockups differ, this text wins. Mockup B showed the painting full-bleed with a dark gradient from the left and light text; the shrink demo showed the painting shrinking onto the site's light paper background.

## Why

The site is for Jak's work, and he wants it to look cool. The 22 historic plates stay at the centre of it: they say "standing on the shoulders of giants". What he dislikes today: when you arrive, the frames at the top are mostly empty and fill in only as you scroll, so you have to scroll through the photos before anything happens. He likes sites with animation and effects tied to scrolling.

## What gets built (Jak's choices)

1. **First screen, "one giant at a time" (mockup B).** One plate fills the whole screen behind his name and tagline, slowly zooming, then fades to the next plate, with its year and a one-line caption. Nothing to scroll through first.
2. **Effect 1, "the painting shrinks into the album".** As the visitor scrolls off the first screen, the full-screen painting shrinks into its own frame in the album grid, and the other plates fade in around it. The album then sits finished on the page as a gallery.
3. **Effect 3, "your work slides in".** In Projects, the items rise into place one after another as they come into view, and a large "80+" counts up from zero.
4. **Effect 4, "the rooms".** In the ten subject rooms, the room's painting stays pinned while the text scrolls past, then gives way to the next room's painting as the wall colour shifts.

5. **Phones: Jak and his work lead, the paintings are atmosphere.** Jak, on the phone view of today's site: "it makes it seem like the photos themselves are the subject"; on his laptop "it's better". The phone rules are below.
6. **Words he can edit himself.** All the page text moves out of `build.py` into one plain text file he can edit in Notepad. He publishes an edit by saying "push"; Claude then rebuilds, runs the checks and the private-name scan, and pushes. (Jak chose this over GitHub publishing his saves directly, which would skip the name scan.)

Not chosen: effect 2, parallax depth (paintings drifting at different speeds). Mockup C, a timeline of plates ending at "2026, Jak Potvin: you are here", is for later, once plates after 1968 are added (Jak: the jump from the 1960s to now is too big).

Jak said to go with Claude's suggestions on the open points and that he will ask for changes after seeing it; those suggestions are marked "Jak took this suggestion" below.

## Goals

- The first second on the site looks striking, and shows a painting, his name and his one-line description with no scrolling.
- His work (Projects) gets its own moment, not just a list.
- The paintings keep their order (oldest first), plate numbers, captions, credits and links to their rooms.
- Works well on phones, and in Chrome, Edge, Safari and Firefox.

## Non-goals

- No new content: no essays, no new plates, no timeline section (C) in this round.
- No parallax (effect 2).
- No changes to the atoms page, the calculator or the list of plates, beyond what shared CSS changes force.
- No framework, no build tooling beyond `build.py`, no third-party JavaScript or CSS.

## Anti-goals (would count as failure even if it technically works)

- Busy or template-looking: several things moving at once, effects that keep going after they have made their point.
- Hiding the work behind the animation, the very thing Jak dislikes today: no screen-holding section the visitor has to scroll through before reaching content.
- Motion sickness or a broken page for visitors whose device asks for less motion.
- A slow first load: 22 full-size images fetched up front, or the first painting arriving late.
- Stutter on phones.

## Constraints

- **The build.** Pages are generated by `build.py` into `site/`; styles live in `site/assets/site.css`. Running the build twice with no changes must produce identical files. Files keep LF line endings. No em or en dashes anywhere on the site or in commit messages.
- **Self-contained.** The site makes no request to any other host (fonts are self-hosted). Keep it that way.
- **Pictures.** Only the existing plates, which are public domain or credited under their licence; credits stay on `plates.html`.
- **Reduced motion.** With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, nothing moves on its own or with scrolling: the first screen shows one painting, held still; the album is shown finished; project items and rooms appear in place; the wall colour may change without a transition, as now.
- **Auto-moving content needs a pause.** The first screen changes on its own indefinitely, so it needs a visible pause control (WCAG 2.2.2), and it should stop changing while the tab is hidden.
- **Publishing.** Nothing goes live until Jak says to push. Before any push, run the private-name scan over the folder and history (README rule), rebuild, and rerun the browser checks.

## Approaches considered (how it gets built)

- **A. The browser's own scroll-linked animations (picked).** CSS scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: scroll()` / `view()`), with a small amount of JavaScript only to measure positions. Smooth because the browser runs them off the main thread; no dependency; matches the site's hand-built style. Browsers without support get a simpler version (see below).
- **B. Hand-written JavaScript on scroll**, as the current album does. Identical in every browser, but more code and more work on every scroll frame, which is where phones stutter. Kept only as the fallback.
- **C. An animation library (GSAP ScrollTrigger).** Most powerful and common on flashy scroll sites, but a large third-party file to self-host and keep updated, for three effects. Rejected.

Jak left the how to Claude; A is Claude's call.

## The design

### First screen

- Full-viewport section. A plate fills it (`object-fit: cover`), with a dark gradient from the left so the text stays readable.
- On it: "Jak Potvin", the existing one-line description, and the existing bio paragraph (on phones too; see Phones).
- Caption block, bottom left: the year in the display face, the event line, the subject (linking to its room) and the short credit ("Plate VI. Magna Carta, 1215"), all from `PLATES` as the readout uses today.
- **Claude's call:** cycles through all 22 plates, oldest first, about 5 seconds each: a slow zoom (scale about 1 to 1.1) and a crossfade of about 1 second. Starts with Plate I. Only the current and the next image are loaded; the first is loaded eagerly with high priority.
- A small pause/play button, and a quiet "scroll" cue at the bottom centre.
- Each plate's crop on phones uses its existing `object-position` value.
- **Jak took this suggestion, plates that don't suit full screen are shown whole.** A plate is shown whole when it is taller than it is wide or square (aspect ratio below about 1.2: Augustine, Caesar, Adam Smith, Goya, Vitruvian Man, Kennedy with von Braun, the Alchemist, Earthrise), or when its file is too small to fill the screen sharply (ENIAC at 630 px wide, Napoleon at 800 px, The First Flight at 1024 px; rule: the source is narrower than about 1400 px, or than the viewport width when that is smaller). Shown whole means: the painting at full height (never upscaled beyond about 1.5 times its pixel size), placed in the right part of the screen, over a dark, heavily blurred and darkened copy of itself that fills the screen; the text and caption stay on the left as for the other plates. All 22 stay in the rotation. The builder decides whether the blurred backdrop is a CSS filter on the small copy or a pre-blurred copy written by the build.
- **Claude's call, the site header on the home page:** it lies over the top of the painting, in light text on the gradient (Writing, Projects, Contact on the right). On the home page only, the header's name link is hidden, because the big "Jak Potvin" heading is right below it; the other pages keep today's header unchanged. The first screen is the full viewport height including the header.
- **Claude's call, image sizes:** `build.py` writes resized copies of each plate (Pillow is already used): a large one for the first screen (long side about 2400 px, never upscaled beyond the source) and a small one for the album grid (long side about 900 px). The album uses only the small copies, so arriving on the page never pulls 22 full-size files. Copies are generated deterministically, so the identical-rebuild rule still holds; the source files in `site/assets/img/` stay as they are.

### Effect 1: shrink into the album

- Scrolling down from the first screen (about one screen-height of scroll) shrinks the current painting from full screen into its own frame in the album grid; at the same time the other 21 plates fade and settle into their frames, and the first-screen text fades out.
- **Claude's call:** cycling stops as soon as the visitor starts scrolling, so the painting that shrinks is the one on screen.
- After the shrink the album is an ordinary section of the page, on the light paper background as today: a finished grid of all 22 plates with their numbers, each linking to its room as now. Scrolling back up reverses the shrink.
- **Claude's call, captions in the album:** one caption line under the grid (year, event, subject link, short credit, the same fields the readout shows today) shows the plate the visitor hovers, focuses with the keyboard, or first taps on a phone (a second tap follows the link). With nothing hovered it shows Plate I. This keeps every plate's year and event reachable, including for reduced-motion visitors, without putting text on every tile.
- The current tall, screen-holding album section and its scroll-driven fill-in are removed.

### Effect 3: Projects arrive

- Each item in the Projects section (the atoms links and the six "Other software" areas) rises and fades into place as it enters view, one after another. Once in place it stays; scrolling back up does not hide it again.
- **Claude's call:** a large "80+" with the label "projects" added above "Other software", counting up from 0 to 80 as it comes into view. The existing sentence "More than eighty projects ..." stays as is.

### Effect 4: the rooms

- On desktop, the rooms become one long section: the plates side is pinned while each room's text scrolls past; when the next room's text arrives, its plates crossfade in and the wall colour shifts (the colours in `ROOM_BG`, as now).
- A room with several plates shows them together in the pinned area, laid out as the room shows them today. A room with no plate keeps its "Plate to come" frame.
- Each room keeps its anchor (`#s-philosophy` and so on), so links from the album and the index still land on the right room.
- **Claude's call:** on phones the pin is dropped: each room shows its text and plates in order, with the colour shift, as today.

### Phones (viewport 760 px wide or less)

Jak took these suggestions; the rule behind them is that on a phone Jak and his work lead and the paintings are atmosphere.

- **First screen:** the painting sits further back (a stronger dark overlay, so it reads as a backdrop); in front of it, his name, the one-line description, the bio paragraph, and two links, to Projects and to Writing, all within the first screen on a Pixel-sized phone (about 412 by 915 CSS pixels). The caption is small: year and event on one or two lines in the small sans face, no giant year. The pause button and the cycle stay.
- **Album:** after the first screen, the 22 plates are one horizontal row the visitor can swipe sideways (scroll snap), oldest first, each tile about 40% of the screen width, with the plate number under it. No shrink effect on phones: the row simply follows the first screen. The caption line below the row shows the tapped plate, as on desktop.
- **No giant year anywhere on phones.**
- **Rooms:** as said above, no pin on phones; each room shows its text and plates in order, with the colour shift.
- **Projects:** the reveal and the counter work as on desktop.
- The test target is Jak's phone, a Google Pixel 10 (Chrome on Android), plus the headless phone-width checks.

### Words Jak can edit himself

- A new file `site-text.txt` in the repository root holds every piece of prose on the home page: the first-screen lines (name, one-line description, bio), the scroll cue, the Writing introduction, each room's line and its "first essay" note, the Projects section (headings, paragraphs, each link's title and description, each area's title and description, the "80+" label), the Contact section, and the footer. Not in it this round: the plate data (`PLATES`: years, captions, alt text, credits, crops), the atoms page, the calculator and the list of plates.
- Format, chosen so it is safe to edit in Notepad: sections headed by a line like `[bio]` or `[room: Philosophy and religion]`, followed by the text as plain sentences; a blank line separates paragraphs; lines starting with `#` are notes to Jak explaining what each section is and where it appears. Links inside text are written as `[words](address)`. No HTML in the file.
- `build.py` reads the file as UTF-8 (with or without a byte-order mark, with Windows or Unix line endings), escapes the text for HTML, and fails with a plain-English message naming the section and line when a section is missing, unknown or empty, or when the text contains an em or en dash (the site's no-dash rule). Typographic apostrophes and quotes are allowed.
- With the file unchanged, the build produces the same pages as before the move (apart from the redesign itself), and two builds in a row are byte-identical.
- The README gains a short "Editing the words" section: open `site-text.txt` in Notepad, change the words, save, and ask Claude to push.

### Checking on Jak's phone before going live

Jak took this suggestion: before any push of the redesign, the new version is served from the laptop on the home Wi-Fi so Jak can open it on his Pixel. Windows will ask once to allow the server through the firewall; that click is Jak's. The server runs only while he is testing and is stopped afterwards.

### One thing at a time

Only one element moves at any moment: the first screen's slow zoom, or the shrink, or a reveal, or a room change. Reveals run once.

### Browsers without scroll-driven animations

Detected with `@supports (animation-timeline: scroll())`. Without it: the first screen still cycles (it runs on time, not on scroll); the shrink is replaced by a simple fade-in of the finished album; project items fade in once using the existing IntersectionObserver approach; the rooms keep today's layout with the colour shift.

## Left to the builder

- Exact timings, easings and distances, within the limits above.
- The album grid layout after the shrink (today's four-column, shortest-column layout is fine to keep), and how to measure the target frame for the shrink.
- How the first-screen markup and the album share the plate data in `build.py`.
- Updating the headless browser checks: the old "album oldest-first at every step" check no longer applies; new checks should cover the first screen, the pause button, the shrink, the reveals, the rooms and reduced motion, at desktop and phone widths, in a browser with and without scroll-driven animation support.

## Change after the build (26 November 2026)

Jak tried the built version and dropped the album from the home page: "i dont like how when you scroll you still see all the photos together like that at the top". Offered (A) remove the album or (B) move it lower, he took Claude's pick, A. So effect 1 (the painting shrinking into the album), the album caption and the phone swipe row are gone; scrolling off the first screen goes straight into Writing. The plates still appear one at a time on the first screen, by subject in the rooms, and with full credits on the list of plates. The album's small copies (`assets/img/small/`) are no longer written.

Later the same day Jak asked whether the plate numbers were needed ("do we really need the plate stuff"). Claude's pick, taken: the site shows no plate numbers anywhere (captions keep artist and title); the list of plates became "Picture credits" (same address, plates.html), with each picture's year beside its full credit, so every licence credit stays one click from every page.
