    What this leaves out

    Most of these make real results worse than the numbers above.

    • Every step is treated as independent, with the same rates. Real builds mix step types with different success rates.
    • Repairs always work and never damage anything, and the check never raises a false alarm.
    • Tool wear is off unless you switch it on. Its wear settings are illustrative, and its published check and repair rates come from a bare metal tip taking images, the nearest data that exists. A tool is either good or worn; real tools degrade in more ways than that.
    • Each measured rate starts from a prepared site, so chaining them is an approximation.
    • Tips never sit idle. Setup, downtime and moving to a fresh tool are not counted; in the published method each tool molecule performs one reaction.
    • Each step places one atom, and a part with a single wrong atom counts as a failure.
    • Success means different things in different counts. Some require the atom to end up in the right place; others only require the right reaction to happen somewhere.
    • Money: there is no cost per tip, per hour or per part.
