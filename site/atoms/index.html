Atom by atom

AI is making design nearly free. Making things is becoming the bottleneck. Atomically precise manufacturing is how the physical world catches up, and it starts with knowing how reliable each atomic step is.

I build the open data and tools that measure that. This page sets out what has been measured so far, using only published counts.

Editing comes before building

Building a whole object atom by atom is decades away. Editing a few atoms in a valuable device is not. Quantum processors are already made by placing single phosphorus atoms in silicon, and a useful device needs only about ten of them in the right places.

The hard part is scale. At 97% per site, a ten-atom device comes out right about three times in four, but a hundred-site array comes out perfect only one time in twenty. That makes checking every site, and reworking the failures, the real job. It is the part I work on.

What would move the question

Whether this becomes manufacturing is unproven. In the papers I have read, none of these has been reported yet. Each would change the picture.

  1. A build of a hundred steps or more, with the outcome of every step counted.
  2. A failed step detected and repaired, with how often the check misses and how often the repair works.
  3. A spent tool reloaded and used again, with counts.
  4. Carbon placed well above 4 K. Silicon removal has been shown at 77 K.
  5. The same step repeated by a second, independent lab.
  6. Raw outcome data released, so others can check the counts.
